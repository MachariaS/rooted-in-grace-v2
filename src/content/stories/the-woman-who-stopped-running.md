A mentor once told me about a young woman who had a very big dream for her life.

She wanted more than a good job. She wanted to build something meaningful. She dreamed of financial stability, successful businesses, land, beautiful homes, cars, a loving marriage and children. She wanted to take care of her parents, create opportunities for her siblings and build a legacy that would give her family a different kind of future.

But there was one thing standing between her and many of the opportunities in front of her.

Fear.

She had once been offered a job and turned it down because she was afraid of what would happen if she left what was familiar. She chose another path instead, only for that chapter to eventually come to an end.

When another opportunity appeared, the memory came rushing back.

*What if it happens again?*

But this time, she decided something.

She would not allow an experience from yesterday to dictate what she did with an opportunity today.

So she applied.

Interestingly, just the day before, she had been in church listening to her pastor speak about the hand of God taking hold of a person and opening doors permanently.

He spoke about doors opening effortlessly.

She didn't know that the very next day, an old colleague would reach out to her about an opportunity she had once been referred to.

It would have been easy to dismiss it.

Instead, she applied.

And that one decision became the beginning of a completely different chapter.

The opportunity opened another opportunity.

Then another.

Her career began to take shape. Her finances became stable. The things she had once written down as impossible slowly became normal.

She built businesses.

She bought land.

She bought cars.

She eventually owned homes that once existed only in her imagination.

But she didn't stop there.

She helped her parents establish businesses of their own. She bought them cars. She gave her siblings opportunities she wished they had never had to struggle to find.

She helped her sister study abroad and connected her brothers to some of the best companies she could find.

And then, somewhere in the middle of building her dreams, she met the man she would marry.

They built a beautiful life together.

A home filled with laughter.

Children running through the rooms.

A marriage built on love, friendship and faith.

One evening, years later, she sat quietly and thought about the woman she used to be.

The woman who was afraid to apply.

The woman who thought one disappointment meant every opportunity would end the same way.

And she smiled.

Because she finally understood that sometimes, the biggest battle isn't getting the door to open.

It's having the courage to walk through it.

Her life had not changed because she suddenly stopped being afraid.

It changed because she stopped allowing fear to make her decisions.

And perhaps that's the lesson in her story.

You can have a history of disappointment and still have a future filled with possibility.

You can have tried before and still try again.

You can be afraid and still move.

You don't need to know exactly where the road leads before you take the next step.

Sometimes, you simply need to trust the One who already knows.

Maybe the opportunity in front of you isn't the whole answer.

Maybe it's simply the first door.

And maybe, on the other side of it, is a life you have been praying for without even knowing how you would get there.

**Don't let an old disappointment make a new decision for you.**

Take the step.

Let God handle the rest.
